# Week 5 — Replicating Search-R1 with LoRA (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, PPO vs. GRPO)

This week replicates two rows of Table 2 of *Search-R1: Training LLMs to Reason and Leverage Search
Engines with Reinforcement Learning* (Jin et al., 2025) on the **Bamboogle** dataset for
**Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct**: the **Direct Inference** baseline and **Search-R1-Instruct**, trained with
both **PPO** and **GRPO**. Unlike the paper, all RL training uses **parameter-efficient fine-tuning
(LoRA)** instead of full fine-tuning. The implementation starts from the TinyZero design (veRL-style
actor / reference / critic, rule-based outcome reward) and adds the three ingredients that define
Search-R1: multi-turn rollouts interleaved with a search engine, retrieved-token loss masking, and an
exact-match outcome reward.

## Method

**Task format.** The policy receives the Search-R1 prompt (paper Table 1) through the Qwen chat
template. It reasons in `<think>`, issues queries in `<search> … </search>`, receives the top-3
passages in `<information> … </information>`, and finally answers in `<answer> … </answer>`. A rollout
may use up to 4 searches plus one final answering turn (≤ 500 new tokens per turn, ≤ 500 tokens per
retrieval, ≤ 3000 response tokens in total). Malformed actions receive the paper's "My previous action
is invalid …" message. Generation stops at `</search>` / `</answer>`; the search request is executed
and the rollout continues (Algorithm 1 of the paper).

**Retriever.** Search-R1's own retrieval server: E5-base-v2 embeddings over the 2018 Wikipedia dump
(\~21M passages), exact flat inner-product FAISS index, top-3 passages.

**Reward.** Outcome-only: 1 if the normalised content of the last `<answer>` exactly matches a gold
answer (lower-casing, punctuation and article removal), otherwise 0. No format reward.

**Retrieved-token loss masking.** Each response is stored as a token list with a parallel mask: 1 for
tokens generated by the policy, 0 for inserted retrieval results and environment messages. The mask is
applied to the policy loss, the KL term and the value loss, and GAE is computed only over policy
tokens (retrieved spans are treated as part of the environment transition). On average only about a
third of the response tokens are policy tokens, so without masking
most of the gradient would come from text the model did not write.

**PEFT.** The actor is Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct with a LoRA adapter (r = 32, α = 64, no dropout) on all
attention and MLP projections; only the adapter is trained. The reference policy is the same network
with the adapter disabled, so no second copy of the weights is needed. For PPO the critic is a second
Qwen2.5-3B backbone with its own LoRA adapter and a zero-initialised linear value head.

**Algorithms.**
*GRPO:* 32 questions × 5 sampled responses per step; advantages are the group-normalised outcome
rewards broadcast to every policy token; clipped surrogate (ε = 0.2) plus a k3 ("low-variance") KL
loss to the reference with coefficient 0.001.
*PPO:* 64 questions × 1 response per step; token-level reward = outcome reward on the last policy token
minus 0.001 · KL (k1) on every policy token; GAE with γ = λ = 1 and whitened advantages; clipped
surrogate (ε = 0.2) and clipped value loss (0.5).
Both use one PPO epoch split into two mini-batches, AdamW, 10 warm-up steps, gradient clipping at 1.0,
sampling temperature 1.0, actor LR 1e-5 and (PPO) critic LR 1e-4. Training data is the merged
NQ + HotpotQA training set (FlashRAG release), as in the paper; Bamboogle is used only for evaluation.

**Compute.** One NVIDIA H200 (141 GB); vLLM generates the rollouts with the current LoRA adapter
hot-loaded each step, while the HF/PEFT model computes log-probabilities and gradients. A GRPO step
took roughly 60 s.

## Discussion

**Direct Inference is reproduced exactly (0.024).** This validates the evaluation pipeline: prompt
format, decoding and the EM metric. Without retrieval the 3B model almost never answers Bamboogle's
compositional questions correctly. Simply prompting the untrained model to use the search engine
already triples the score (0.072), and RL then improves it by another factor of four.

**GRPO reproduces, and exceeds, the paper's number after only 50 steps.** All three GRPO runs follow
almost the same reward curve (0.09 → \~0.35), and both complete runs end at \~0.30 EM, above the paper's
0.232. The middle plot shows *why* the score improves: the number of valid searches per rollout rises
steadily from \~0.9 to \~1.8, i.e. the policy learns to decompose multi-hop questions into several
queries. This is the core behaviour Search-R1 claims RL can teach. Training was too short to reach
the regime where the paper reports GRPO reward collapse; we saw no sign of it (format errors stay low
and the reward keeps rising).

**PPO learns at first and then degrades on Bamboogle.** Up to step 20 PPO behaves like GRPO
(0.24–0.26 EM, close to the paper's 0.264). In the complete run, Bamboogle EM then falls to 0.168
(step 40) and 0.145 (step 50). A drop of 0.11 is about three standard errors on 125 questions, so it
is unlikely to be noise alone. The training logs of that run point to three causes:

**The PPO / GRPO comparison is confounded by sample budget.** With the defaults used, GRPO processes
160 rollouts per step and PPO only 64, so PPO's policy gradients and critic targets are noisier.
Together with the very short training (50 vs. 500–1000 steps in the paper), this is the most likely
reason our ranking (GRPO > PPO) is the reverse of the paper's (PPO slightly > GRPO). Our observation
that GRPO converges faster than PPO does agree with the paper.
